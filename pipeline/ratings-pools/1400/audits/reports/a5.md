## Common checks and methodology

### Integrity first

The outer compressed archive matched the supplied SHA256 before extraction. `SHA256SUMS` matched all twelve inner archives; the six other packages were integrity-checked only, not semantically audited. Every inner archive's provenance was checked against its compressed byte length and hash, exact regular-file path set, and every file's byte length and SHA256. Absolute/traversal paths and non-regular/non-directory members (including symlinks and hard links) were disallowed. The extracted bytes of all six audited packages, evidence, and state files were checked again against those manifests before signoff.

Package manifests and canonical evidence digests were independently recomputed. Generator/oracle source hashes, test pair hashes, authored-pair digest, sandbox implementation hashes, oracle-index identity/source binding, and reviewer independence were checked. Hashes alone were not treated as correctness evidence.

### Statements, strict validation, and independent answers

Each statement was read directly. All six ask for a unique integer answer under whitespace-insensitive token comparison. None is interactive, requires judge file I/O, permits multiple answers, uses floating tolerance, or needs a special checker. This semantic/checker check passes even for packages rejected on other grounds.

The auditor wrote a separate strict input validator and separate solvers, without executing or importing the worker generators/recipes on the host. Every supplied official, open-r1-generated, worker-generated, and sample pair was checked. CRLF was explicitly recognized during validation, not mistaken for an algorithmic discrepancy. Validation included exact counts/line structure, numeric ranges, sortedness, alphabets, permutations, per-order distinctness, and the adjacent-step guarantee in 602B.

| Problem | Independent solution | Different small-instance check |

| 604B | Pair opposite ends of the smallest `2*(n-k)` bells, and account for the largest single | Exhaustive one/two-bell box allocations |

## Rejected packages

### 604B — REJECTED: false adversarial coverage reason

Coverage adversarial test **0041**, labeled **“pairing must match smallest with largest paired”**, is:

```text
6 4
1 2 3 4 50 60
```

The answer is **60**, dominated by the largest single bell. Pairing `(1,2)` and `(3,4)`, leaving `50` and `60` single, is also optimal and does **not** perform the claimed smallest/largest paired matching. Thus the claimed necessity is literally false and the named test does not discriminate this pairing error. The independent exhaustive check agrees with answer 60. Other boundaries/max-size claims are accurate, including 0035/0036, all three maxima 0037–0039, and largest-single domination 0040.

All five accepted sources pass. All five incorrect sources have genuine defects and remain wrong after LF normalization, but this cannot override the coverage failure:
